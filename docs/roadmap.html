Fair federated learning on real hospital data

Four hospitals train one heart-disease model without sharing patient records. We test whether that shared model treats women and men fairly, whether fair-FL methods such as FairTrade (AAAI 2024) still work on messy real data, and how to fix them where they break.

FLamby · Fed-Heart-Disease 740 patients · 4 hospitals sensitive attribute: sex updated 4 Oct 2026
The research question
Does fair federated learning survive real cross-silo medical data, where some hospitals have almost no patients from the protected group?

The goal: a rigorous, reproducible project that extends FairTrade, strong enough to propose as a Master's project or thesis in the L3S Trustworthy AI Lab.

    Federated normalization experiment

    DoneWeek 1

    Hospitals share only counts, sums and sums of squares so that everyone scales features the same way. A third variant first imputes the cholesterol values coded as 0, to rule out a missing-data shortcut.

    Accuracy rises from 0.704 to 0.765, in 10 of 10 seeds. The cholesterol shortcut explains almost none of it. The model also becomes less fair to women: SPD worsens in 10 of 10 seeds. A preprocessing choice alone shifts the accuracy–fairness trade-off.
    run_fedavg.py --normresults/fedavg_heart_*.csv
  6. 5

    Leave-one-hospital-out test

    DoneWeek 1

    Train on three hospitals and test on the fourth, which the model has never seen. If the normalization gain came from the model recognizing hospitals, it should vanish here.

    It doesn't vanish. At unseen hospitals with balanced disease rates (Cleveland, Hungary), balanced accuracy still improves by about 3 points (p ≈ 0.03), half the original gain. At hospitals where most patients are sick, the accuracy jump is the model predicting "disease" more often: at Long Beach, balanced accuracy actually drops by 6 points. From now on, balanced accuracy is a headline metric and imputation is the default.
    scripts/run_loho.py

    Local and pooled baselines

    DoneWeek 1

    Completed the baseline set: one model per hospital, and one model trained on all data pooled together, which is the upper bound FL tries to reach. Both got the same fairness metrics.

    All 100 local-model accuracies match FLamby's published files exactly. Pooled training reaches 0.796, the ceiling.   8. 7

    Port FairTrade to FLamby

    DoneWeek 1

    Rebuilt FairTrade as clean, tested code on the real hospital loaders: each hospital trains with a fairness penalty, and Bayesian optimization searches for the best accuracy–fairness trade-offs. It uses size-weighted averaging and tunes on a validation split, never the test set. The port fixes six defects in the challenge code, including test-set tuning and a reference point that made the "Bayesian" search random.

    FairTrade removes the sex gap in prediction rates (SPD −0.34 → +0.02) at a cost of 7 balanced-accuracy points, and nearly triples the Pareto-front hypervolume (0.047 → 0.133), in all 10 seeds. Key finding: women's disease rate is 25%, men's 60%. Forcing equal prediction rates over-diagnoses women, so sick women are now detected more often than sick men (EOD +0.14). For diagnosis, equal opportunity is the right fairness target, not demographic parity.
    fairfl/fairtrade.pyscripts/run_fairtrade.pytests/test_fairtrade.py
  9. 8

    Our fix: equal opportunity and pooled group totals

    DoneWeek 1

    A 2×2 experiment: two fairness targets (equal prediction rates vs equal detection of sick patients) × two penalty scopes (each hospital alone vs group totals pooled across hospitals). The pooled version shares only 4 numbers per hospital per round. Tests confirm it equals the penalty on all data combined.

    Equal opportunity is the better target: it shrinks the detection gap for sick women (EOD −0.19 → −0.04) without over-diagnosing them, keeps more accuracy (0.761 vs 0.732), and covers significantly more of the trade-off space (p = 0.04). Null result: pooled group totals don't beat FairTrade's per-hospital penalty here. 96% of women in the training data are at Cleveland and Hungary, so their local penalties already carry the fairness signal. The failure mode we target isn't strongly present in this dataset.
    fairfl/fairtrade.pyscripts/compare_fairtrade.pyresults/fairtrade_*_<notion>_<scope>.csv
  10. 9

    Stress test: where per-hospital fairness fails

    DoneWeek 1

    Re-split the same patients into 10 clients with increasing sex segregation, up to clients that hold only women or only men, as at a veterans' hospital or a women's clinic. Compared per-hospital (FairTrade) and pooled (ours) fairness penalties at each level, 10 seeds.

    When every client holds a single sex, FairTrade's penalty switches off and its results equal FedAvg exactly. Our pooled version still works: hypervolume +0.081 for demographic parity (10/10 seeds, p < 0.001) and +0.031 for equal opportunity (p = 0.006). Below that, the two are on par: one client with both sexes is enough for FairTrade. Our version is never significantly worse, so it is a safe default that also covers the case where FairTrade breaks.
    fairfl/partition.pyscripts/run_stress.pyresults/stress_s*.csv

Rigor rules

Never a single number

Every result uses 5–10+ seeds and reports a mean with a 95% confidence interval. Method comparisons use paired tests on the same seeds.

Preprocessing fit on train only

Normalization and imputation statistics come from training data only. No tuning on the test set; FairTrade will be tuned on a validation split.

Undefined means n/a

If a group or class is missing from a test set, the metric is reported as n/a, never silently as 0.

Check before claiming

Every gain gets a control: the imputation run for the cholesterol shortcut, and leave-one-hospital-out for hospital recognition.
